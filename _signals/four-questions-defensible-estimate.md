The argument over "that wasn't in the quote" happens at collection time, which is the worst
possible moment. It is almost always traceable to something that was never written down.

## 1. What is explicitly excluded?

Not "standard exclusions apply". The actual list, in plain language a homeowner understands.
Permit fees. Correction of pre-existing non-compliant work. Concealed conditions not visible
at estimate. Drywall repair, patching and painting. Work on equipment not named in the scope.

An exclusion that obviously does not apply to this job makes the whole list read as
boilerplate, which trains customers to skip it. Edit the list per job.

## 2. What access do you need?

Clear access to the equipment. Someone on site. Pets secured. Parking. Whether a service will
be interrupted and for roughly how long. This is the cheapest line item you will ever write
and it prevents the most expensive thing that happens to a schedule — a wasted trip.

## 3. What conditions would change this scope?

Name the realistic possibilities behind whatever you are opening up. Undersized existing
wiring. Non-compliant prior work. Corroded pipe. No accessible shut-off. Insulation of unknown
type.

Naming them specifically is more honest than "unforeseen conditions" and considerably more
useful when one of them turns up.

## 4. What happens if the customer declines?

State it before they need to ask: what state you would leave the site in, and what would
remain unresolved. A customer who understands the decline path trusts the change order more.

---

None of this requires new software. It requires the four answers to exist in writing before
the estimate goes out.
